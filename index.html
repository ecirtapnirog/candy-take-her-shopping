Take her shopping

The smallest testable slice of the EverAI case's lead initiative: gifting rebuilt as a scene in the chat. Variant A is today, as seen on 21 Oct 2026: asked to go shopping, the character improvises a picture with nothing to buy; the member gives from the gift shelf in the chat (27 gifts, 5–999 tokens) or leaves for the Candy Shop to buy an outfit, gift it, come back and ask her to wear it. Variant B is the scene: she offers three real gifts or outfits at real prices, priced to the member, the member pays in place, and she wears it or shows it. Characters, pictures and payments are placeholders; the gifts, outfits and prices are Candy's, 21 Oct.

AvaFlirting · level 4

1 · Outcome: grow token spending

The case's illustrative baseline, not an estimate of EverAI's business: it is there to check orders of magnitude. B-lines are from the main document; G-lines are what members spend today on gifts and outfits, computed from the assumptions in section 4.

2 · Opportunity

"I want to give her something that matters in our story."

32% of members have sent her a gift, but from a shelf of emoji: the gift is chosen alone and the story barely notices. 15% bought an outfit within weeks of the Shop opening, but the Shop is outside the chat. Asked to go shopping on 21 Oct, three characters refused, stalled or sent a picture with nothing to buy (Achievements page and site checks, 21 Oct).

3 · Solution: the scene, against today

Rule 1 · her three picks. The model writes her lines; this rule decides what she may offer: one item at the member's usual price, one a step up, one cheaper, and at least one outfit she can wear while any are left.

0 = never bought. Most members sit between 50 and 150.

The rule for six kinds of member

Rule 2 · the top-up in the scene. Reused from the AI-led prototype: the smallest pack covering the item plus three days at the member's pace; the cheapest that covers it is always shown.

On the baseline: ordinary payer 4–5, heavy payer 11–26.

The rule on four items, for this member

4 · Assumptions, and what the test reads

Premium Plus members who open a chat during the test, split 50/50: 9,850 a side, 14 days. Each row is something that must be true for B to be worth building. Rows marked "simulated" are drawn from the assumptions at the bottom, to show how the result would be read; the others are read in the real test only.

Where the main measure comes from, arm by arm
Assumptions behind the simulation — change them to stress-test the decision

A reproduces today. Outfits: 16% × 1.5 × 38% ≈ 0.091 per member a fortnight ≈ 2.4 a year, the floor read from the Achievements page (15% bought one, 7% three). Gifts: 16% × 1.5 × 62% ≈ 0.149 a fortnight, i.e. the 32% who have sent a gift giving about one a month. Prices: an outfit is any of the 16 (average ~108); a cheap gift is chosen more often than a dear one, averaging ~100 of a shelf that runs 5–999. B is the case's base case: a little more buying, picks priced to the member; in B the usual pick is taken half the time, the cheaper and the step up a quarter each. Tokens at $0.10 (B9).
