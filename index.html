Take her shopping

The smallest testable slice of the EverAI case's lead initiative. Variant A is today's path, as seen on 21 Oct 2026: asked to go shopping, the character improvises a picture with nothing to buy, so the member finds the Shop, buys, gifts, comes back and asks her to wear it. Variant B is the scene: she offers three real outfits at real prices in the chat, the member pays in place (with a sized top-up if short), and she wears it. Characters, pictures and payments are placeholders; the catalogue and prices are Candy Shop's, 21 Oct.

AvaFlirting · level 4

1 · The question

Does a shopping scene in the chat get members to spend more tokens on outfits than the Shop alone?

Why ask. 15% of members have bought from the Shop since it opened, 7% three times. Yet asked to go shopping, the characters offer nothing to buy: the member has to leave the story, find the Shop, buy, gift, come back and ask.

What it sits on. On the case's illustrative baseline the Shop sells about $3.0M a year: 116,000 subscribers × ~2.4 outfits a year × ~108 tokens × $0.10. The bar: +20% tokens spent on outfits per member over 14 days, worth about $0.6M a year at full rollout.

3 · What B offers, and why it matters

Her three picks. The model writes her lines; a rule decides what she may offer, so every offer is a real outfit at its real price.

The rule for every kind of member

The top-up inside the scene. The AI-led prototype's rule, reused: the smallest pack that covers the outfit plus three days at the member's pace, with the cheapest that covers it always shown.

The rule on four outfits, for this member

4 · The readout: A against B

Premium Plus members who open a chat during the test, split 50/50: 9,850 a side, 14 days. The members are simulated from the assumptions at the bottom, so this shows how the decision would be read, not a result.

Assumptions — change them to stress-test the decision

A = 7% × 1.3 ≈ 0.09 outfits per member per fortnight ≈ 2.4 a year: the floor read from the Achievements page (15% bought one, 7% three), the same rate the $3.0M uses. B = 10% × 1.4: the base case in the case document, about +40% in tokens. In A members buy any catalogue outfit (average 108 tokens); in B they take the cheapest pick 30% of the time, the usual 50%, the step up 20%. Tokens at $0.10. Not simulated, and watched as guardrails in the real test: pack revenue per member over 30 days, refunds and disputes, generation cost per session.
