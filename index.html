Take her shopping

The smallest testable slice of the EverAI case's lead initiative. Variant A is today's path, as seen on 21 Oct 2026: asked to go shopping, the character improvises a picture with nothing to buy, so the member finds the Shop, buys, gifts, comes back and asks her to wear it. Variant B is the scene: she offers three real outfits at real prices in the chat, the member pays in place (with a sized top-up if short), and she wears it. Characters, pictures and payments are placeholders; the catalogue and prices are Candy Shop's, 21 Oct.

Member

Usual price 0 means the member has never bought an outfit; she then anchors on the catalogue median.

Her three picks, live

One outfit at the member's usual price, one a step up, and always the cheapest she doesn't own. The model writes her lines; this rule decides what she may offer, so every offer is a real item at its real price.

Top-up inside the scene

Reused unchanged from the AI-led prototype: the smallest pack covering the outfit plus 3 days at the member's pace; 350 (no bonus) becomes 550 (+10%) if the member spends 550 within a week; the cheapest covering pack is always shown.

What the readout will look like

Premium Plus members who open a chat during the test, split 50/50: about 9,850 a side (17% of ~116,000 payers). Synthetic members with assumed behaviour, over 14 days. The main measure is tokens spent on outfits, the brief's metric; outfit count is watched beside it. It shows how the decision is read, not a result.

Assumptions behind the simulation

Calibrated to Part 1. A: 7% of members buy an outfit in two weeks at 1.3 each, about 2.4 outfits a year, the floor read from the Achievements page (15% bought one, 7% three since launch). B: 10% at 1.4 each, which comes out near +40% in tokens, Part 1's base case, because her picks shift some buying to the cheapest outfit. Outfits per buyer are 1 plus a Poisson draw. Prices in A are any catalogue item (average 108 tokens); in B the cheapest pick is taken 30% of the time, the usual 50%, the step up 20%, anchored on a usual price drawn from the catalogue. Tokens are valued at $0.10. Rollout value assumes all ~116,000 subscribers behave like the Premium Plus test group. The guardrails (pack revenue per member over 30 days, refunds and disputes, generation cost per session) need real data.
